# Understanding the Carat-Price Chart

This chart shows the relationship between **diamond carat (weight)** and **price**, separated by **clarity level**.

## Dots
Each dot is one diamond. **X-axis:** carat. **Y-axis:** price. **Color:** clarity (`I1`, `IF`, `SI1`, `SI2`, `VS1`, `VS2`, `VVS1`, `VVS2`). The spread of dots at any given carat shows that price varies a lot even among diamonds of the same size and clarity.

## Lines
The colored lines are **smoothed trends**, not connections between individual diamonds. They summarize the general pattern:

> As carat increases, price generally increases - but the shape and steepness of that increase differs by clarity.

`IF` (internally flawless) trends higher than `I1` at most carat weights, for example.

## Shaded areas
The gray bands are **confidence intervals around the trend line**, not the range individual diamond prices fall into (that's what the dots show). Wider bands mean more uncertainty. Bands widen noticeably above ~1.5 carats for several clarity groups, since there are fewer diamonds that large in the data, the trend there is less reliable.

## When lines cross
A crossing means the **estimated trend lines** for two clarity groups are approximately equal at that carat value - not that any diamond is mispriced. Diamond price depends on more than carat and clarity (cut, color, fluorescence, etc.), so trends can legitimately swap order at different weights. After a crossing, one group's estimated average price may run higher than the other's for a while.

## Main takeaway
1. **Bigger diamonds cost more, on average.**
2. **Clarity matters**, but its effect on price isn't the same at every carat weight.
3. **The relationship isn't strictly linear**, and individual diamond prices vary substantially even within the same carat/clarity group.
